## What it means to me…

Leadership is something that can involve being at the top of a hierarchy but also is the responsibility of everyone in an organization. It involves taking responsibility for your own actions and their consequences as well as advocating for others and trying to improve both yourself and the world in whatever way you can. To be an equitable, just and culturally competent leader you have to extend yourself outside of your comfort zone an allow yourself to be corrected, and therefore leadership not only does not mean having the most power but actually means being able to give up power in order to achieve a goal.

## How I have demonstrated this…

Working on SCRLC Create and also on the proposal to improve the Wayland Free Library services for the immigrant community have both involved leadership within the Steuben County Library System. I also have mentored the disadvantaged when I was working as a physics researcher in the early 2010s. In our classwork at Syracuse University we have been consistently challenged both to embody leadership and to examine both our philosophy of leadership and that of the library as an institution. I have led the development of systems for interacting with users and collaborating with my classmates in the form of a MySQL scheduling platform, categorizing metadata for datasets, creating ideas for new technological implementations, and planning a reference implementation for disadvantaged students. But ultimately the most important work is looking at how libraries are dysfunctional workplaces with known biases and asking what needs to be done to move forward to a better world together, by ceding power to the impacted parties that have suffered for so long.

## What I plan to do in the future…

In the future I will continue to lead in the Steuben County library system by improving the computer systems, applying for grants and building platforms for collaboration like SCRLC Create. By attending online conferences and events like NYS Advocacy day I will keep up with trends in the library world. And by listening to marginalized voices I can be a force for change to empower the powerless and not reinforce a paradigm where power over others justifies injustice.
