## Summary 

Pursuing an MSLIS after working for almost a decade in software engineering disciplines like data engineering, ML Ops and DevOps seems like a large change, but is fundamentally motivated by my own personal philosophy. Society improves when data is available for everyone, not just for the limited powerful few. Embracing that philosophy alone is not enough given the rapid changes in technology. Having a solid background in the technologies that are largely hoarded by the powerful is necessary to bridge the gap that makes data democratization possible. That is the passion that has motivated me both in my career as a data professional and also in my work as a library clerk and as an MSLIS student.

## Reflection

I have shown my commitment to data democratization in many ways through my MSLIS and library clerk work. Both my IT management CAS and my coursework on reference, metadata, library management and library marketing have been laser focused on analyzing the needs of the community, not just the technical specifications. This includes considering the ways that digital access is both liberating for underserved communities and also the way that accessibility and digital access can be improved. It also includes reflecting on both my own biases and the biases of the library profession, diving into the way I think about creating metadata and meeting the reference needs of the patron beyond knowing about the materials and focusing on knowing how to meet them where they are. My personal focus with the SCRLC Create project and the Wayland Free Library digital improvement grant proposal work that I have done with the director Elisabeth Cowley are a core of action I have already implemented to show the impact that the learnings from my course work and my own personal reflection on the needs of the community.

## Future goals 

My future goals include deepening my AI literacy practice, continuing to work with SCRLC to digitize records and build consortia among libraries, and maintaining my engagement with data platforms and technologies as they evolve. The Wayland Free Library digital improvement grant proposal has already established a concrete roadmap: integrating teleconferencing infrastructure, launching an immigration-focused online book club in 2027, and improving both language and disability accessibility on the library website in accordance with W3C standards. In parallel I will pursue NYS library certification and continue building the professional network and credentials that position me to move into a librarian role when the market allows.
